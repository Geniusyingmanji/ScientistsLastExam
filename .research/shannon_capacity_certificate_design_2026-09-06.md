## Why this problem

Shannon posed the zero-error capacity of the five-cycle in 1956; Lovász answered it in 1979 by
inventing `theta`. For every odd cycle from the seventh onwards the capacity is still unknown, and
what exists is an interval whose top has not moved since 1979 and whose bottom moved **three times
in July 2026** (arXiv:2607.21517, 2607.27869, 2607.29681). That is the rare combination this
benchmark wants: a problem a field has worked on for seventy years, still open, still actively
contested, and with both sides of the answer expressible as a finite object a grader can check
exactly.

## What is scored, and why the interval rather than the bound

The product is a certified interval `[|S|^(1/k), b]`, and the score is its width.

* Below: a set of codewords, no two adjacent in the strong power. Verification is `|S| * 3^k`
  dictionary lookups — a membership test, not a search.
* Above: a rational Lovász matrix and a rational bound with `b*I - A` positive definite, decided by
  exact rational elimination.

Scoring the *interval* rather than the lower bound alone is what makes the upper certificate
load-bearing: a sloppy `b` widens the interval and costs score, an invalid one scores zero. It is
also the scientifically honest object — nobody knows the capacity, so the deliverable is the
interval you can prove.

## Why exact elimination is affordable here and was not in BellBoundCertificate

`BellBoundCertificate` had to take its certificate **as the squares**, because a submitted matrix's
size is the candidate's choice and rational elimination's cost grows with the input's values rather
than its size — a well-formed submission could hold the grader indefinitely. Here the matrix is
`n x n` with `n` fixed by the instance at 7, 13, 19 or 23 and every entry capped, so the cost is
bounded by the instance. Measured: 0.184 s to verify all four free-set certificates (the largest a
1521-codeword power-4 code), 0.011 s to refuse a 4000-codeword power-5 submission.

## Measurements that changed the design

**The suite went from one cycle to four.** A single `C7` instance is most of the way solved by a
recallable parameter: sweeping `(modulus, multiplier)` rediscovers Polak and Schrijver's `(382, 7)`
in about fifteen seconds, and prune -> extend -> plateau then reaches 366 of the known 367, which is
~0.92 of that instance. No comparable published parameter exists for C13, C19 or C23 at the allowed
powers, so three quarters of the score has to be derived rather than recalled. This is disclosed in
the card at `novelty_risk: high` rather than argued away.

**C11 was cut.** Its power-3 circular code reaches 148 codewords, `148^(1/3) = 5.28978`, against a
published 5.294502 — a 5 per cent band with nothing in it.

**C13 moved from power 4 to power 3.** The power-4 sweep reaches 1513 codewords and
`1513^(1/4) = 6.2362`, which is *below* what squaring the classical two-coordinate set already
proves (`1521^(1/4) = 6.2450`). 245 at power 3 gives 6.2569, above it. Taking the largest allowed
power is not automatically right.

**The diagonal shift is useless as a symmetry.** Quotienting `C_n^{box k}` by `v -> v + (1,...,1)`
gives zero internally-independent orbits, because that shift is adjacent in every coordinate. The
group that works is generated by a codeword whose multiples avoid `{0,+1,-1}^k`; that quotient gives
343 for `C7` at power 5, the same as a dimension-3 linear code over `GF(7)`, and both are dead ends.

**Five natural constructions score exactly zero on C7 at power 5**, because all five land below the
free bound the shipped power-4 set already proves (3.2237098):

| construction | codewords | bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| best of ten random greedy | 246 | 2.9932 |
| plateau walk, five restarts of two minutes | 337 | 3.1975 |
| composition of the best power-2 and power-3 codes | 330 | 3.1841 |
| dimension-3 linear codes over `GF(7)` | 343 | 3.2088 |
| plateau on the 2401-coset cyclic quotient | 343 | 3.2088 |

The `GF(7)` codes are the sharpest illusion: maximal, no vertex addable, no `(1,1)` swap improving
them, and worth nothing.

**The C7 free bound was held at the published 108 and then found.** The first search runs reached
only 106 at power 4. Rather than lower the floor to what the package could build, the floor stayed
at the published power-4 value and the search was pushed: six seeded ILS runs, three reached 108,
three stalled at 106. So the zero of the scale is a witness the oracle's own test accepts, not a
citation.

**The classical-floor budget was raised** from 500k draws to three restarts of 2M after 500k reached
125 of the 126 codewords for `C23` at power 2.

## The upper side derives `theta` rather than reading it

For the odd cycle the Lovász matrix can be taken circulant, collapsing the semidefinite program to
one free variable: the eigenvalues are `n - 2 + 2x` and `2(x-1)cos(2*pi*k/n)`, and the best `x` is
where the largest cross. The reference bisects that crossing on rationals with a capped denominator
and then bisects `b` upward until the exact test accepts, landing ~1.5e-6 above
`n cos(pi/n)/(1+cos(pi/n))` for all four cycles without ever naming it. Agreement with the closed
form is the check that both are right.

## Getting the reference inside the evaluation timeout

First measurement: 171 s (C7), 54 s (C13), 212 s (C19), and the C23 sweep over a 170-wide modulus
window ran past 400 s on its own - together well past the 720 s timeout. Two changes, one accepted
and one rejected.

**Rejected: halving the sweep by the multiplier symmetry.** The code for multiplier `m` and for
`m^{-1} mod n` are exact coordinate-reversals of each other - `{(t, tm, tm^2)}` under `s = tm^2`
becomes `{(s, s*m^{-1}, s*m^{-2})}` - and coordinate reversal is an automorphism of the strong
product that commutes with the coordinate-wise rounding. So the two codes have the same independence
number and skipping one of each pair looked free. It is not: `prune` is a greedy heuristic whose
result depends on tie-breaking, not only on the isomorphism class, and on `(n, m) = (382, 5)` the
pair gives 118 and 116. The sweep keeps the maximum, so skipping would sometimes discard the better
of the two. Measured before it went in, which is the only reason it did not.

**Accepted: vectorising the code construction.** The sweep is dominated by *building* candidate
codes, not by pruning them - about five milliseconds each in pure Python, tens of thousands of them
per window. The arithmetic is exact integers well inside int64, so the numpy form returns the same
set; the task's tests check that against a plain loop on five parameter sets. The C19 sweep went
from 219 s to 48 s with an identical result, `807` codewords at `(807, 19)`.

Windows are set by the rule "bracket `published_target ** power`", which is principled rather than
fitted to where the winner turned out to be, and a test asserts both that each window brackets its
target and that C7's window still contains the published `(382, 7)` - if it ever stops containing
it, the reference silently gets worse.
