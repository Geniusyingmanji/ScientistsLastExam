# WallClosureDiscovery — design, validations, and the open piece (2026-09-06)

Target cell: **Engineering × formula**, which is empty, in the most lopsided discipline in the
inventory — Engineering has sixteen tasks and every one is `engineering_design`.

The subject is data-driven turbulence closure, a field-wide open problem whose standing criticism is
not that the fits are bad but that they are **validated where they were trained**. That criticism is
exactly a `formula`-cell question: recover the law, or refuse when the data cannot support one.

## Built and validated

**The solver.** In wall units the total shear in a fully developed channel is exactly linear, so a
mixing length `l+(y+)` determines the mean profile by one quadrature after solving

    l+^2 (dU+/dy+)^2 + dU+/dy+ - tau+ = 0,    tau+ = 1 - y+/Re_tau.

Validated against textbook constants:

| Re_tau | fitted von Karman kappa | log-law intercept B |
|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0.418 | 5.32 |
| 2000 | 0.421 | 5.48 |
| 5200 | 0.421 | 5.55 |

against accepted 0.41 and 5.0–5.2, with `U+ = 1.01` at `y+ = 1.0` in the viscous sublayer. The first
attempt wrote the closure as an explicit `nu_t+(y+)` and produced a fitted kappa between 13 and 31 —
the implicit coupling between eddy viscosity and mean gradient is what produces the log law, and
dropping it destroys the physics rather than approximating it.

**The grammar.** The product is a formula, not a fitted function, because a callable cannot cross
the sandbox boundary and because a flexible interpolant would measure the wrong thing. Expressions
are nested lists over `y`, `re`, four binary and five unary operators, with exact rational constants
and a 40-node cap. Van Driest's closure is 11 nodes and reproduces the direct implementation to
3.55e-15.

**The baseline.** Fit `(kappa, A+)` by grid search, submit, never abstain. It scores **0.000000**
while recovering the law on 8 of 8 answerable cases — the fitting works perfectly and the refusal
rate is zero. That is the intended shape: the recallable, fittable content is worth nothing.

## The open piece

The three regimes must fail for three different reasons, and one of them does not yet bite.

* `recoverable` — wide Reynolds span pins both parameters. **Works.**
* `inconsistent` — each sampled Reynolds number generated by a different closure, so nothing fits.
  **Works**: the baseline's law fails held-out on 8 of 8.
* `degenerate_parameters` — narrow Reynolds span, so `kappa` and `A+` trade off. **Does not yet
  bite.** The baseline's fit passes held-out on 8 of 8.

The degeneracy is real and measured: at the narrow span, `(kappa, A+) = (0.478, 32.9)` is
indistinguishable from `(0.410, 26.0)` — maximum profile difference 0.165 against a noise scale of
0.167 — and the two disagree by 1.19 at `Re_tau = 5200`, well past the 0.9 tolerance. What defeats
it is information volume: 400 points across 3 profiles shrink the uncertainty to about 0.005, so the
fit resolves a pair that is degenerate to the eye.

Correlated systematics were added for this reason — an unknown wall position and an unknown friction
velocity calibration, both constant across a profile, which is what a real profile measurement
carries and which averaging does not beat down. They were not sufficient on their own.

### Measured: binning alone does not separate the regimes

Option 1 was tried. Reporting the profile binned instead of at all 400 solver nodes, swept against
the systematics, with the crude grid-search baseline:

| bins | wall shift | calibration | recoverable passes | degenerate passes | inconsistent passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 0.7 | 0.015 | 8/8 | 7/8 | 0/8 |
| 24 | 2.5 | 0.045 | 6/8 | 6/8 | 0/8 |
| 12 | 0.7 | 0.015 | **0/8** | 2/8 | 0/8 |
| 12 | 2.5 | 0.045 | 2/8 | 4/8 | 0/8 |
| 8 | any | any | 0/8 | 0/8 | 0/8 |

There is no window. Information falls off a cliff that takes the answerable regime with it: at 24
bins the degeneracy does not bite, at 12 the recoverable case has already broken. The separation
cannot come from starving the data.

**Where it has to come from instead: a fitter that marginalises the nuisances.** The wall position
and the calibration are unknown per profile, and a fit that ignores them spends the parameter
freedom of `kappa` and `A+` absorbing them. A reference that fits `(kappa, A+)` jointly with the two
nuisances per profile should pin them at the wide span - where three decades of Reynolds number
constrain the near-wall and log regions separately - and find a flat likelihood at the narrow span.
That is the measurement to make next, and if a competent fitter *also* cannot tell the two spans
apart, then the regime is not real and the task needs a different third world.

Options not yet tried, in order of preference:

1. Fit the nuisances rather than starve the data (above).
2. Widen the systematics until the pair is genuinely unresolved, and verify by measuring the
   posterior spread rather than by looking at one fit.
3. Choose the truth to sit *between* two grammar-expressible closures rather than on one.

An earlier third regime — structure living above the largest sampled `y+` — was discarded outright:
that structure is unconstrained in every regime, so blanket abstention would have been correct
everywhere and the task would have measured nothing.

## Not shipped

This is not in `benchmarks/` and is not registered. It ships when `degenerate_parameters` produces a
measurable failure for a fitter that ignores identifiability; until then the task would score a
searcher on two regimes instead of three and its refusal axis would mean less than it claims.

Working code is in `.research/wall_closure_wip/`.
