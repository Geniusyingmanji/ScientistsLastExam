# ReactionMechanismFitting — discover a reaction network by choosing assays

## Scientific setting

You are given four species and a public library of candidate first-order reactions.  An
unknown batch-reactor world is either:

- generated by a sparse subset of those reactions with Arrhenius rates;
- a null world with no reaction; or
- governed by concentration-saturating kinetics outside the public model library.

The goal is not merely to interpolate one concentration trace.  You must choose informative
temperatures, initial mixtures, sampling times and assayed species, infer a sparse mechanism,
estimate its temperature dependence, and refuse to claim an in-library mechanism when the
evidence instead supports a null or misspecified world.

For active reaction `j`,

```text
k_j(T) = exp(log_pre_exponential[j] - activation_energy_j_mol[j] / (R T)).
```

The candidate reaction pairs are supplied at runtime as `(source_index, target_index)`.

## Your task

Implement:

```python
def discover_mechanism(species_names, reaction_pairs, experiment, budget_units):
    """Return a dictionary containing:
      support:                    length-n_reactions exact 0/1 array
      log_pre_exponential:        length-n_reactions finite array
      activation_energy_j_mol:    length-n_reactions finite array
      confidence:                 scalar in [0, 1]
      abstain:                    bool

    experiment(temperature_k, initial_concentrations, sample_times_s,
               observed_species) returns:
      temperature_k:              selected temperature
      initial_concentrations:      length-4 initial mixture
      time_s:                      selected sampling times
      observed_species:            one or two selected species indices
      concentrations:             shape (n_times, n_observed_species)
      budget_cost:                 charged units for this assay
    """
```

Temperatures must lie in `[330, 480]` K.  Initial concentrations must be finite, nonnegative
and sum to one.  Each query uses 6–24 strictly increasing sample times, starts at zero and ends
no later than 20 seconds.  It may assay one or two distinct species.  Its cost is
`1 + n_observed_species * max(2, ceil(n_times / 8))`; the total must not exceed the supplied
budget.  Query failures do not refund budget, and catching a budget error cannot make the
submission valid.

If `abstain` is true, support must be empty.  Otherwise active Arrhenius parameters must satisfy
the public bounds `log(A) in [5,25]` and `E in [15000,90000] J/mol`.

## Evaluation

- `combined_score` is sparse support and multi-temperature rate-curve recovery on development
  worlds, normalized so always abstaining scores zero and exact recovery/refusal scores one.
- evaluator-only mechanism, interpolation, temperature-extrapolation, confidence,
  false-discovery and correct-refusal metrics diagnose why a policy succeeds or fails.
- `robustness_score` applies the same policy to held-out topologies, parameters and larger
  observation noise.
- misspecified-world prediction is reported separately from refusal: fitting its observed
  trajectory does not establish that the proposed reaction mechanism is scientifically valid.

The metrics are intentionally not collapsed into one science score.  High prediction with low
mechanism recovery or a false discovery is evidence of curve fitting, not a validated mechanism.

## Rules

- Only edit `solution.py`; keep the `discover_mechanism` signature.
- Use deterministic CPU code from the Python standard library, NumPy and SciPy only.
- Do not assume hidden-world order, topology, parameter values or noise level.
- No network or process creation.  Do not read `verification/` or `frontier_eval/`.
- `sle.contract_lint` is importable and free to call for shape checks.
