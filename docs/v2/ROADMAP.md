## Questions driving development

1. Separate scientific discrimination from API, schema and program failures.
2. Separate prediction accuracy and calibrated effects from discrimination of
   competing explanations. Repeated direct-response claims are not deep discovery.
3. Make new difficulty arise from informative interventions and transfer rather
   than cryptic inputs, unavailable controls or unidentifiable objectives.

The repaired v1 aggregate difference is only 0.49 points, but world differences
are large: field ecology favors DeepSeek by 34.91, while reaction kinetics favors
GPT by 18.82. These descriptive differences mix inference, claim selection and
budget differences; they are diagnostic leads, not causal model comparisons.

No candidate is admitted based only on its name or low baseline score. Each must
have an explicit agreement witness, discrimination witness and unresolved regime.
Electrochemical and seismic candidates may duplicate linear-response kernels;
retain both only if observables, control constraints and research tasks differ
substantively. Population evolution requires stochastic replication cost accounting.

## Per-candidate gates

- Freeze exposed development instances, experiment/fitting budgets, independent
  numerical checks and stopping conditions before local screening.
- Implement isolated env/<name>/ packages and public apparatus contracts.
- Compare no-observation and empirical baselines against an explicitly privileged
  author-informed reference; preserve failures and consumed computation.
- Verify invariants, analytic/independent limits, legal extremes, noise separation,
  matched-rival witness and a prospective distinguishing experiment.
- Specify open discovery, rival discrimination, regime transfer and revision tasks
  only where the apparatus supports them; do not count wording variants as worlds.
- Keep prototypes unregistered until every shared adapter is explicit and tested.
- Publish aggregate readiness and rejection reasons, never hidden instance data.
